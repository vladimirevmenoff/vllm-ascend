What gets computed, and what moves

Generating one token means multiplying an 8 KB vector against 8.95 GB of weights. Almost nothing is computed; almost everything is moved. That ratio is the entire performance story, and once you see it the measured numbers stop being surprising.

1 · The shape of the problem

At batch size 1 every weight is loaded from DRAM, used for exactly one multiply-accumulate per element, and thrown away. A 4096 × 12288 projection reads 50 MB of weights to turn a 8 KB vector into a 24 KB vector. The chip's compute units are idle waiting for bytes — the profiler marks 96 % of decode time as memory-bound, and the pipe mix shows 84 % MTE2, the pipe that moves data from DRAM.

2 · One decode step, start to finish

Follow a single token. Bytes in red are DRAM traffic — the thing that actually costs time. Shapes are per token; activations stay in on-chip buffers throughout.

  1. Embedding lookup

    Gather one row out of the 248 320 × 4096 table. The table is 2.03 GB but you touch one row.

    reads 8 KB · writes 8 KB · h = [4096] fp16

  2. × 24 — linear-attention layer

    RMSNorm the 8 KB vector, project it to q, k, v and a gate (4096 → 12288, 50.3 MB), run a width-4 causal convolution over its own 3-token history, then update the recurrent state S ← (diag α)S + βkᵀv and read out out = qS. The state is 32 heads × 128 × 128 in fp32 — 2.1 MB, read and written every token. Gate, then project back (4096 → 4096, 16.8 MB).

    weights 67.4 MB · state 2.1 MB in + 2.1 MB out · per layer

  3. × 8 — full-attention layer

    Project q (with its output gate), k and v (41.9 MB), rotate 64 of the 256 head dims, append k and v to the KV cache, attend over everything seen so far, project out (16.8 MB). At 2048 tokens of context the cache read is 8.4 MB per layer — real, but two orders of magnitude below the weight traffic.

    weights 58.7 MB · KV cache 8.4 MB @ 2k ctx · per layer

  4. × 32 — SwiGLU MLP, after every layer of either kind

    Project up to 2 × 12 288 (100.7 MB), apply silu to one half and multiply, project back down (50.3 MB). This single block is 151 MB per layer, 4.83 GB per token — more than half of all traffic.

    weights 151.0 MB · per layer · 54 % of the step

  5. Final norm and vocabulary projection

    One last RMSNorm, then multiply the 8 KB vector by the 4096 × 248 320 lm_head. It was left unquantized, so it is read at fp16: 2.03 GB for one matrix, 18.9 % of the step. Out comes a 248 320-wide logit vector — about 1 MB in fp32, the largest activation anywhere in the model.

    weights 2.03 GB fp16 · writes ~1 MB of logits

  6. Sampling

    Greedy is an argmax over those 248 320 values. Top-k / top-p instead has to sort or partition the whole vector — the one place in the whole step where vocabulary size, not weight size, sets the cost.

    reads ~1 MB · writes 1 token id

Two things fall out of this table. First, W8A8 moves 1.76× fewer bytes and runs 1.63× faster — the gap is the lm_head, identical in both columns, diluting the win. Quantize it and the total drops to 8.10 GB, predicting ≈ 55 ms. Second, both configurations land at 146–158 GB/s against a measured 188 GB/s copy ceiling, so there is nothing left in the kernels; only fewer bytes or larger batches can help.

4 · Why prefill behaves nothing like this

Prefill reads the same 8.95 GB of weights, but does 2048 tokens' worth of work with them — arithmetic intensity jumps from 1.8 to roughly 3600 FLOP per byte. DRAM stops being the constraint, and the bottleneck moves to how fast the chip can issue work.

The measurement confirms it: in prefill the scalar pipe is 45 % of core time and the matrix unit only 12 %, the reverse of decode. The time goes into thousands of tiny tensor operations, not into the GEMMs. In particular the linear-attention layers cannot use their recurrent form — they switch to a chunked algorithm whose triangular solve issues 1512 small reductions (63 iterations × 24 layers), and that cluster alone is 35–41 % of prefill.

5 · The counter-intuitive part

Everything that makes this model architecturally interesting is free. The gated delta rule — 24 layers of it — costs 0.92 ms of a 62 ms step. Attention over the whole context costs 0.64 ms. The recurrent state and the KV cache together are under 2 % of traffic.

What costs time is the least interesting matrix in the model: a stack of plain feed-forward projections, plus one enormous vocabulary matrix. When you optimise this model you are almost never optimising attention — you are managing how many bytes of MLP have to cross the memory bus per token.
